## Draft 1 — Reddit technical-infrastructure post

Candidate audience: data-center or cloud-infrastructure community after rule and moderator approval.

**Title**

What to look for in the next hyperscaler earnings: availability, power and deployment cadence — not just CAPEX

**Body**

The most useful signal in the next US technology earnings cycle may not be the headline CAPEX number by itself.

For AI infrastructure, I am watching five disclosure points:

1. **Capacity availability.** Are cloud providers still supply-constrained, and does management describe when additional AI capacity can be brought online?
2. **Power and site readiness.** Does management discuss grid connection, generation, power procurement or the time needed to energize new campuses?
3. **Deployment cadence.** Is spending translating into commissioned capacity, or is it still primarily construction and equipment delivery?
4. **Cooling and network bottlenecks.** High-density AI clusters make thermal design, rack integration and interconnect delivery increasingly material.
5. **Contracts and utilization.** Long-duration leases, anchor tenants and GPU supply commitments can be more informative than generic “AI demand” commentary.

For example, the current public project pipeline includes a planned 1GW AI campus in Childress, Texas, Meta’s 5GW Hyperion target in Louisiana, and a 175MW long-term campus lease in Georgia. The underlying question is how quickly announced capacity becomes energized and usable compute.

I work on IDC Atlas, a source-linked public information project. I am sharing this as research context, not investment advice. If moderators permit external links, I can add the source list and the earnings/CAPEX calendar in a comment; otherwise I will keep the discussion fully here.
